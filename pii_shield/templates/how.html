How detection works

PII Shield uses patterns and checksums, not a trained model. That makes results predictable and explainable, and it also sets the limits of what it can find.

What happens to your text

Each data type has a pattern that finds candidates. A validator then checks the candidate: Aadhaar numbers go through the Verhoeff checksum, cards through the Luhn checksum, dates must exist on the calendar, and IP address parts must be 0 to 255. Matches that cannot be real are dropped. Matches that fail a softer rule are kept and marked low confidence so you can check them yourself.

When two patterns overlap, the more specific one wins. A card number is never also reported as a phone number, and a UPI ID is never also reported as an email address.

Redaction styles

Partial mask
Keeps a small, low-risk part, such as the last four digits of a card or the email domain, so people can still tell records apart.
Type label
Replaces each item with its type, such as [EMAIL] or [PAN]. Best for sharing logs and tickets.
Solid block
Replaces every character with a block. Reveals nothing, including length patterns you may want to keep.

What it does not find

Names, street addresses, job titles, medical details and anything written in free-form prose are not detected. Numbers with spaces or typos in unexpected places can be missed, and ordinary numbers can occasionally match. Treat the scan as a first pass, not a guarantee.
